| 01 | 0:00 | What you just saw is a mushroom. And I designed it to be Malenia. |
| 02 | 0:08 | Malenia is probably the most hated, and at the same time the most respected boss FromSoftware has ever made. I'm making my own metroidvania, and I asked myself one question: can you put that feeling into a 2D game? |
| 03 | 0:32 | First, we need to understand why she works. Malenia has two things that make her unique. The first is a mechanic that's easy to explain, but completely changes how we fight her. Every time she hits us, she heals part of her health. Even when we block with a shield. So playing too defensively can backfire: while we're just trying to survive, Malenia is healing back all the damage we worked so hard to deal. |
| 04 | 0:58 | But what truly defines this fight is her dance. An attack that can shred us in seconds, and forces us to react in a completely different way from the rest of her moveset. Malenia leaps into the air, hangs there for a moment, and then dives at us with a flurry of extremely fast slashes that can chase us even when we try to run. |
| 05 | 1:29 | And those two ideas were exactly what I wanted for my enemy. Punish us for getting hit, and have an attack that, the moment it starts, makes us think only one thing. |
| 06 | 1:44 | And that's how we got to this creature. Completely harmless at first glance. A mushroom. The idea was to turn it into a fast, extremely aggressive enemy, but above all, to make fighting it defensively a really bad decision. So the first thing I took from Malenia was her ability to heal every time she lands a hit. In this case, the mushroom heals 150% of the damage it deals to us. |
| 07 | 2:09 | But the real problem showed up when I tried to bring the dance into a fully 2D space. I didn't have a 3D camera, or a chain of sword animations I could use to create that feeling. So I rebuilt the attack with projectiles. The mushroom jumps, hangs in the air for a moment, and then fires three consecutive bursts that chase the player. Each one is made of seven neon slashes coming from different angles, mixing arcs with crossing cuts to keep us reacting all the time. And none of these slashes are drawn as traditional sprites. The whole effect is generated in code, which lets me control their position, direction and behavior during the attack. |
| 08 | 2:53 | After the third burst, the mushroom finally lands and stays vulnerable for a few seconds. A small window to pay back all the damage we've been dodging until now. |
| 09 | 3:05 | And since I was trying to recreate Malenia, obviously I couldn't leave out her Scarlet Bloom. The mushroom jumps until it almost disappears off screen, while a pink circle marks where it's going to land. A moment later, it slams into the ground with a huge explosion. |
| 10 | 3:20 | So far, it seemed to work pretty well. But then came the part of development that usually doesn't look so pretty when you show the finished result: testing whether what you just made can actually be dodged. And no. This time, the problem wasn't just that I'm a little bad at the game. |
| 11 | 3:41 | My character uses a dash to dodge attacks, and after using it, he needs exactly 0.78 seconds before he can dash again. The problem was that I had set the bursts of the dance to arrive every 0.58 seconds. So I had built an attack where the next burst could reach you before your dash was ready again. |
| 12 | 4:03 | And that's exactly when I understood one of the most important things about the original fight. Malenia's dance can look completely impossible the first time we see it, but behind all that chaos there's a solution the player can learn and repeat. The difficulty can be brutal, but there always has to be an answer. |
| 13 | 4:18 | After a lot of tweaks, deaths, and one version that literally couldn't be dodged, I finally got something much closer to what I was looking for from the start. A boss that can feel completely absurd the first time we face it, but slowly starts to make sense once we understand how it works. |
| 14 | 4:35 | And I think that's the most important thing when you design a hard boss. Make it brutally hard, but fair at the same time. When we die, we should know what we did wrong. Every attack should have an answer. And the boss should never need to cheat to kill us. |
| 15 | 4:53 | Because my goal with this project, which will probably take me a few years to finish, isn't just to make my own metroidvania. One of my biggest goals is to create some of the best bosses we've ever seen in the genre. Bosses that can kill us twenty, thirty, or fifty times if they need to, but after every attempt, we feel a little bit closer. And when we finally beat them, the feeling isn't just relief that an unbearable fight is finally over. It's that huge satisfaction of knowing we won because we learned to play better. |
| 16 | 5:28 | Because for me, a good hard boss isn't the one that makes you die a lot. It's the one that makes you want to try again after every death. And that's exactly the kind of boss I want to create in Jekyria. |
